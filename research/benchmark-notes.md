## Benchmark Patterns

The strongest robotics and STEM team sites tend to include:

- An immediate mission statement and clear call to join or sponsor.
- Team history and values, especially FIRST-style gracious professionalism.
- Program pathways for mechanical, software, strategy, business, and outreach.
- Robot archive pages that preserve institutional memory.
- Sponsor tiers with concrete benefits.
- Events and outreach pages that show community impact.
- Media galleries and competition storytelling.
- Lightweight admin/content workflows so students can maintain the site.

### Curated benchmark reference set

1. Apple — extremely narrow top-level surface, strong hero-led hierarchy, and product-focused secondary clusters.
2. Stripe — compact global navigation with product, solutions, resources, and pricing grouped into clear buckets.
3. GitHub — a small set of primary tabs paired with rich nested sections instead of a long flat menu.
4. Linear — product-led navigation with fewer visible tabs and deeply organized supporting destinations.
5. OpenAI — editorial-first homepage structure with focused navigation and clearly separated secondary links.
6. NASA — large-scale mission content organized into major hubs rather than many competing top-level links.
7. Notion — minimal top-bar structure, deliberate CTA placement, and lightweight secondary actions.
8. Microsoft — simpler top navigation with grouped solutions, support, and product pathways.
9. Tesla — concise flagship navigation with dominant action areas and low visual clutter.
10. Figma — streamlined global nav, product-led storytelling, and grouped resource links.

The site now follows the same playbook: a small primary menu, a collapsed overflow area for lower-priority pages, and more deliberate grouping in the header.

Benchmarks reviewed included Team 254, Stormgears FRC 5422, FRC 2813 Gear Heads, Team 751 barn2robotics, FRC Team 180 S.P.A.M., PCHS Robotics, Team 1661, Team 4272 Maverick Robotics, Washburn Millerbots, and FIRST/WPILib ecosystem pages.
